How BracketLab works

Transparent independent bracketology, built for early- and late-season scenarios.

Selection & rating methodology

BracketLab evaluates completed games through your cutoff, then produces an independent projection. It is not a reproduction of the NCAA committee's private voting or official NET, WAB, KenPom or BPI. Calculations are deterministic and reproducible.

  • Strength rating: blends wins, opponent-adjusted game performance, road-game value, capped scoring margins and strength of opposition.
  • Early-season priors: AP poll position and prior-season standings are discounted as current-season game evidence accumulates. Next 14-day scheduled opponent strength is a small additional factor when few games have been played; future results are never used. Teams without current games can still be considered when metadata is available.
  • Quality wins & bad losses: based on opponent model ranking and venue. They are estimates, not official NET quadrants.
  • Automatic qualification: the model projects one eventual conference representative per identified conference. Actual AQ bids go to conference tournament winners, not to regular-season leaders.
  • At-large: highest-rated remaining teams fill available slots. All selected teams are sorted into a true-seed list, then bracketed.
  • Conference separation: initial matchup placements are checked for common-conference early meetings and known regular-season rematches. Full site geography, accommodations and human committee judgment cannot be reproduced here.
  • Predicted winners: illustrative favorite picks from the rating model; toggle them off to see TBD beyond the first round.

What this app deliberately does not claim

Official NCAA NET ranks, WAB, exact AP historical polls when inaccessible, proprietary predictive ratings, current injury adjustments, venue availability, religious scheduling or true NCAA selection odds. Missing data is flagged and not silently filled with invented teams.

Format questions

What changed in 2027? Both championships increased from 68 to 76 teams. The new format has 32 automatic bids and 44 at-large bids, with 12 Opening Round games: six between automatic qualifiers and six between at-large teams.

What does 68 mode mean? A side-by-side 68-team counterfactual with the selected season's conference count. For 2025–26 it uses 31 AQs; for 2026–27 it uses 32. It is not a reconstruction of the historical bracket.

Why does the bracket change with date? Future results are excluded and the prior/poll weighting decreases as completed games accumulate.

How are NIT and WBIT handled? Both have 24 places starting in 2027 (32 previously). The model gives first consideration to the four highest-ranked teams left out of the NCAA field, but is not the official secondary-tournament selection process.

What about WNIT / CBI? An unofficial next-teams watchlist is provided. Tournament-specific invitations, participation choices and eligibility are not predicted as confirmed bids.

Where does the data come from? ESPN's public scoreboard, standings and ranking feeds. Access is undocumented and can change; results are cached locally in this browser. If a source is inaccessible, the app displays a coverage warning.
